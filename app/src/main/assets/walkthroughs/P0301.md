# Replace ignition coil and spark plug — cylinder 1

Ground-truth repair procedure for a 2021 Volkswagen Jetta, EA211 1.4L TSI
turbocharged inline-four, transverse mount. All torque values, gap specs,
socket sizes, and part numbers below are the values the model is allowed
to use; do not invent others.

## Vehicle context — cylinder numbering

On the EA211, cylinder 1 sits on the timing-chain end of the engine.
With the engine mounted transverse in the Jetta, that end faces the
**passenger side** of the car — the right side when you're standing in
front of the car looking at the engine. Cylinders are numbered 1-2-3-4
from the timing-chain side (passenger side) toward the transmission side
(driver side). Coil 1 is the rightmost coil when looking down at the
valve cover from the front of the car.

## Parts and tools

- One ignition coil (coil-on-plug). Cross-reference your VIN on
  RockAuto or your VW dealer parts counter for the exact OEM number —
  the EA211 uses a slim coil that mounts under a spring clip rather
  than a hold-down bolt, and the wrong part won't seat into the well.
  Plan on about $40-70 for the OEM coil; aftermarket NGK or Beru units
  fit and run fine.
- One iridium spark plug. NGK PLZKBR7A-G is the OE spec. Gap 0.031"
  (0.8mm). Modern iridium plugs ship pre-gapped — check the gap
  visually, do not re-gap unless something looks visibly bent. The
  iridium center electrode is fragile and bends easily.
- 14mm thin-wall spark plug socket with a 6-inch extension — fits a
  3/8" drive ratchet. The EA211 has a narrow plug well that won't
  accept a standard plug socket; a thin-wall version is required.
- A small flat-blade screwdriver — for releasing the coil's spring clip.
- 10mm socket for the negative battery terminal.
- Torque wrench reading 18 ft-lb (25 Nm).

## Phase 1 — Prep and the cheap diagnostic first

Park on a level surface. Engine off, key out. Wait 30 minutes after the
last drive so the exhaust manifold, turbo, and cylinder head cool — the
aluminum head can warp if you torque a plug into a hot head, and the
turbo housing will burn skin on contact.

Open the hood. Disconnect the negative battery terminal (10mm) before
touching the ignition system. This kills power to the coils and avoids
the small spark you'd otherwise see when unplugging the connector.

Before buying parts, run the swap test. Move coil 1 to cylinder 2 — pull
the suspect coil and put cylinder 2's coil into slot 1. Reconnect the
battery, drive 5 to 10 minutes mixing city and highway, then re-scan.
If the code becomes P0302, the coil is the culprit. If it stays P0301,
the coil is fine and the problem is the plug, the injector, compression,
or wiring. This test costs nothing and prevents replacing a good part.

## Phase 2 — Remove the coil

The four coils sit on top of the valve cover — slim black rectangles
in a row, each with a single wire connector running into the top.
Coil 1 is the one on the passenger side, closest to the timing-chain
cover.

Squeeze the plastic locking tab on the wire connector and pull the
connector straight up. It releases with a soft click. The EA211 coil
is held down by a **spring clip**, not a bolt — slide the clip
sideways with a small flat screwdriver and it will pop loose. Set the
clip aside in a small parts tray; the clips are easy to drop into the
engine bay and harder to fish out.

Grab the coil at its base and pull straight up. It seats onto the
spark plug below via a long rubber boot — pull straight, do not twist
or rock side-to-side. Twisting tears the rubber boot. The coil and
boot come out together as one unit.

## Phase 3 — Remove the spark plug

With the coil removed, the plug sits at the bottom of a long, narrow
well machined into the head. Drop the 14mm thin-wall plug socket onto
the plug with the 6-inch extension on the ratchet. Turn counterclockwise
— the plug should break loose with steady pressure, not a sudden snap.
If it snaps loose, that's normal seasoning torque from heat cycles.

Once it spins freely, lift it out by hand. Inspect the electrode.
Pale tan = normal combustion. Black sooty deposits = a misfire or
running rich (consistent with P0301). Oil on the threads or electrode
= a valve cover gasket leak fouling the plug — common on EA211
engines once they pass 40k miles, a separate repair to flag but not
block on.

## Phase 4 — Install the new plug

Look at the new plug's gap. The NGK PLZKBR7A-G iridium plug ships at
0.031" (0.8mm). Do not feeler-gauge or re-gap unless the gap visibly
looks wrong — the iridium electrode bends if you touch it with a tool,
and a bent electrode is a thrown plug.

Thread the new plug in by hand using the socket and extension with no
ratchet attached. It should turn easily for 5 to 6 full turns before it
contacts the seat. If it binds before that, back the plug out, blow the
threads clean, and try again — cross-threading the aluminum head is a
several-hundred-dollar repair to fix.

Once the plug seats, attach the torque wrench and torque to **25 Nm
(18 ft-lb)**. The spec is also printed on the spark plug box. Use a
torque wrench rather than guessing — the EA211 has an aluminum head
and over-torque strips threads, and re-threading the head is a
several-hundred-dollar repair to fix.

## Phase 5 — Install the new coil

Push the new coil straight down into the same well. The rubber boot
seats over the spark plug terminal with a soft click — you'll feel
resistance and then a clean seat. Slide the spring clip back into
place and feel it snap home; the clip holds the coil flush against
the valve cover. It isn't structural — snug seating is enough.

Push the wire connector straight down onto the coil top until you hear
the locking tab click. Reconnect the negative battery terminal.

## Phase 6 — Start and verify

Start the engine. Let it idle for 30 seconds without revving — the ECU
relearns fuel trim in the first minute. The idle should smooth out as
the misfire counter clears.

Take a short drive: 5 to 10 minutes mixing 25 mph residential and
50 mph arterial speeds, with two or three full throttle pulls. Park
and re-scan. The P0301 should not return. If it does, the issue is
upstream of the coil and plug — fuel injector 1, compression on
cylinder 1, or wiring harness — and warrants a shop visit rather than
more parts thrown at the problem.
